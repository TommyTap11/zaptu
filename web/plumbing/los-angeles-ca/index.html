Common reasons people call

  • Leaks. Dripping faucets, leaking supply lines, water stains on ceilings or walls, or a meter that moves when nothing is running. Low pressure together with rusty water in an older Los Angeles home can point to aging supply lines.
  • Water heaters. No hot water, not enough hot water, leaking tanks, or strange noises. If the tank is being replaced, ask how it will be braced for earthquakes.
  • Clogs and slow drains. Kitchen sinks, toilets, tubs, and floor drains that back up or drain slowly.
  • Sewer lines. Gurgling drains, sewage odors, or backups in more than one fixture at once.
  • Burst pipes. Sudden water where it should not be. Shut off the main valve first if you can.

Things to know in Los Angeles

  • Some older homes still have galvanized steel supply lines, which can narrow with rust and reduce water pressure.
  • Know where your main water shutoff is. It matters for leaks and for earthquake preparedness.
  • If your water heater is being replaced, ask about the earthquake bracing or strapping that California requires.

What happens when you call

  1. Your call goes to a call-routing partner that works with independent plumbing companies in the Los Angeles area.
  2. If a participating professional is available for your ZIP code, you are connected so you can describe the problem.
  3. You and the plumbing company discuss timing, the work, and any costs directly. Zaptu is not part of that agreement.

Availability depends on your ZIP code and the time of day. Zaptu does not perform plumbing work, does not send a named company, and does not book appointments.

Before you call

  • Find your main water shutoff and close it if water is actively leaking.
  • Have your ZIP code ready, plus a short description of what you see and hear.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 first.
