Common reasons people call

  • Leaks. Dripping faucets, leaking supply lines, water stains on ceilings or walls, or a meter that moves when nothing is running. For Port Saint Lucie homes on a slab, describe any warm or damp spots on the floor and changes in the water bill.
  • Sewer lines. Gurgling drains, sewage odors, or backups in more than one fixture at once. If the home is on septic, slow drains throughout the house are worth mentioning on the call.
  • Clogs and slow drains. Kitchen sinks, toilets, tubs, and floor drains that back up or drain slowly.
  • Water heaters. No hot water, not enough hot water, leaking tanks, or strange noises.
  • Burst pipes. Sudden water where it should not be. Shut off the main valve first if you can.

Things to know in Port Saint Lucie

  • Some homes in the area use septic systems. Say whether you are on septic or city sewer when you call.
  • An unexplained jump in the water bill, or a warm or damp spot on the floor, can point to a leak under the slab.
  • Heavy rain can overload drains and sewer lines. Describe where any backup appears.

What to tell the plumber

  • Your ZIP code, the type of home (house, condo, apartment), and its rough age if you know it.
  • Where the water shows up, whether it drips or sprays, and whether the water meter moves with every fixture off.
  • Whether the home is on city sewer or septic, which fixtures back up, and any sewage odor.
  • Which drains are slow, whether more than one backs up at the same time, and what you have already tried.

When to call right away

  • Water you cannot stop, even with the fixture valve closed.
  • Sewage coming up through a drain or toilet.
  • Water near outlets, panels, or light fixtures.
  • No water at all to the house.

A slow drip or a single slow drain can usually wait for a time that suits you, but mention how long it has been going on.

What happens when you call

Availability depends on your ZIP code and the time of day. Zaptu does not perform plumbing work, does not send a named company, and does not book appointments.

Before you call

  • Find your main water shutoff and close it if water is actively leaking.
  • Have your ZIP code ready, plus a short description of what you see and hear.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 first.
