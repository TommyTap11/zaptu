Common reasons people call

  • Burst or frozen pipes. Sudden water where it should not be. Shut off the main valve first if you can. In a Kansas City cold snap, pipes along outside walls and in unheated basements are the usual trouble spots.
  • Sewer lines. Gurgling drains, sewage odors, or backups in more than one fixture at once. Water coming up through a basement floor drain during heavy rain can point to the sewer line.
  • Clogs and slow drains. Kitchen sinks, toilets, tubs, and floor drains that back up or drain slowly.
  • Leaks. Dripping faucets, leaking supply lines, water stains on ceilings or walls, or a meter that moves when nothing is running.
  • Water heaters. No hot water, not enough hot water, leaking tanks, or strange noises.

Things to know in Kansas City

  • If your home has a sump pump, check that it runs before the spring storm season.
  • Pipes along exterior walls and in unheated basements are more likely to freeze on cold nights.
  • Older homes may have cast-iron or clay drain lines. Mention the age of the house if you know it.

What to tell the plumber

  • Your ZIP code, the type of home (house, condo, apartment), and its rough age if you know it.
  • Which faucets have no water, where that pipe runs (outside wall, crawl space, garage), and whether the main is shut off.
  • Whether the home is on city sewer or septic, which fixtures back up, and any sewage odor.
  • Which drains are slow, whether more than one backs up at the same time, and what you have already tried.

When to call right away

  • Water you cannot stop, even with the fixture valve closed.
  • Sewage coming up through a drain or toilet.
  • Water near outlets, panels, or light fixtures.
  • No water after a freeze, which can mean a frozen or split pipe.

A slow drip or a single slow drain can usually wait for a time that suits you, but mention how long it has been going on.

What happens when you call

  1. Your call goes to a call-routing partner that works with independent plumbing companies in the Kansas City area.
  2. If a participating professional is available for your ZIP code, you are connected so you can describe the problem.
  3. You and the plumbing company discuss timing, the work, and any costs directly. Zaptu is not part of that agreement.

Availability depends on your ZIP code and the time of day. Zaptu does not perform plumbing work, does not send a named company, and does not book appointments.

Before you call

  • Find your main water shutoff and close it if water is actively leaking.
  • Have your ZIP code ready, plus a short description of what you see and hear.
  • If you smell gas, leave the building and call your gas utility or 911 first.
