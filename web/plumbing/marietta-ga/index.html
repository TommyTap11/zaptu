Common reasons people ask for a plumber

  • Sewer lines. Gurgling drains, sewage odors, or backups in more than one fixture at once. In Marietta neighborhoods with large trees, roots are a frequent suspect when backups keep returning.
  • Clogs and slow drains. Kitchen sinks, toilets, tubs, and floor drains that back up or drain slowly.
  • Leaks. Dripping faucets, leaking supply lines, water stains on ceilings or walls, or a meter that moves when nothing is running. Clay soil that shifts between wet and dry spells can stress buried supply lines.
  • Water heaters. No hot water, not enough hot water, leaking tanks, or strange noises.
  • Burst or frozen pipes. Sudden water where it should not be. Shut off the main valve first if you can.

Things to know in Marietta

  • Clay soil swells when it is wet and shrinks when it dries, which can stress buried water and sewer lines.
  • Mature trees in established neighborhoods can send roots into older sewer lines. Mention backups that keep returning.
  • Hard freezes are not a regular event here, but they do happen. Know where your main shutoff valve is.

What to tell the plumber

  • Your ZIP code, the type of home (house, condo, apartment), and its rough age if you know it.
  • Whether the home is on city sewer or septic, which fixtures back up, and any sewage odor.
  • Which drains are slow, whether more than one backs up at the same time, and what you have already tried.
  • Where the water shows up, whether it drips or sprays, and whether the water meter moves with every fixture off.

When to act right away

  • Water you cannot stop, even with the fixture valve closed.
  • Sewage coming up through a drain or toilet.
  • Water near outlets, panels, or light fixtures.
  • No water after a freeze, which can mean a frozen or split pipe.

A slow drip or a single slow drain can usually wait for a time that suits you, but mention how long it has been going on.
