Things to know in Dallas

  • On the hottest afternoons, a working system can still fall behind the thermostat setting. Say how far the indoor temperature is from the setting and whether it catches up overnight.
  • Outdoor units sit in the sun and collect cottonwood fluff, grass clippings and dust. Mention whether the outdoor coil looks clogged or the fan is not spinning.
  • Many homes use a heat pump or a gas furnace for winter. Say which one the home has if known, and whether the problem started during a cold snap.
  • Spring storms and hail can damage outdoor units. Note any storm damage when you describe the problem.

Before someone follows up

  • If there is a smell of gas, leave the home right away and call the gas utility or 911 from outside. Do not stay inside to look for the source.
  • If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, get everyone out, including pets, and call 911 from outside.
  • If there is a burning smell, or water near the unit, switch the system off at the thermostat or at the breaker.
  • A few checks come first: thermostat mode and batteries, the breaker, and whether the filter is packed with dust. Mention what was already checked.
  • In extreme heat or cold, take care of people who are vulnerable to the temperature before worrying about the equipment.
